Experiential Immanence

A philosophical system of unrestricted suspension

Experiential Immanence undertakes an inquiry into appearing while suspending every claim to knowledge. It neither affirms that reality can be known nor concludes that knowledge is impossible. Its descriptions and distinctions are provisional and subject to the same suspension they express. Here, immanence names a restriction of inquiry, not a claim that experience is all that exists.

  1. Appearing as the point of departure

    The inquiry begins with whatever presents itself, provisionally termed . Perceiving, feeling, remembering, imagining, judging, and doubting are admitted for consideration without requiring to what they seem to disclose. This beginning is a methodological undertaking, not a proof that exists as an ultimate reality. A denial of may itself be considered as an appearance; this does not establish an indubitable foundation.

  2. Experience and the scope of inquiry

    is used here as a name for . Its use posits neither a substance, a container for representations, nor an experiencer distinct from what appears. The inquiry attends to what appears and how it appears, termed its . Givenness implies neither certainty nor something delivered to a recipient. Whether exhausts reality, whether anything exists independently of it, and whether that distinction is ultimately adequate remain suspended.

  3. Phenomenal parity

    No mode of is excluded merely because it is doubtful, conceptual, imagined, or mistaken. This concerns admission to inquiry; it does not declare appearances identical or their claims equally credible. Perception may appear compelling, recollection uncertain, and imagination freely variable. These differences may be described without being accepted as guarantees of truth. Interpretation and are likewise admitted; the inquiry requires no foundation of uninterpreted sensation.

  4. Epoché

    The governing practice is : withholding to claims presented as knowledge or as settled accounts of reality. Suspension neither affirms a claim nor affirms its negation. It need not eliminate belief, conviction, or the felt inevitability of ; these may themselves be considered as appearances. A claim may guide deliberation and action provisionally without being endorsed as knowledge. Suspension applies to claims of fallible knowledge as well as claims of certainty. Whether suspension is complete or consistently maintained is itself open to examination.

  5. Description and commitment

    A description attempts to articulate what appears and how it appears. An ontological or epistemic commitment endorses a claim about what exists or what is known. The distinction concerns how a formulation is used, not two separate regions of reality. “There seems to be a cup” can function as a provisional description; it is not thereby an infallible report. Descriptions remain selective, interpretive, and revisable. Neither their wording nor their apparent fidelity exempts them from suspension.

  6. Intentionality and horizon

    Where has the character of being of or about something, this directedness is termed . What appears is considered in the manner in which it appears: as perceived, remembered, imagined, anticipated, or otherwise presented. A cup’s visible side may be seen while its reverse is anticipated without being seen. Such anticipated or implicit aspects constitute a . Neither directedness nor is taken to settle whether an independent object exists. The inquiry does not require every appearance to have an object.

  7. Manifestation and reflection

    An may be described as occurring without a separate act of observing it. provisionally names this character; it does not establish that every knows itself or contains an inner witness. Reflection occurs where itself becomes a theme of attention. It supplies no guaranteed access to an unchanged original: attending, recollecting, and describing may alter what is subsequently presented. Neither reflection nor apparent immediacy confers infallibility.

  8. Perspective and subject

    Where a appears, it is described without concluding that a underlies it. Bodily orientation, a sense of ownership, and an explicit thought of “I” need not be treated as the same appearance. Their relations remain matters for inquiry. The presence of an “I”-thought does not establish an independently existing experiencer; its absence does not establish without a subject. A substantial self, a subjectless reality, and other interpretations remain unendorsed possibilities rather than conclusions of the system.

  1. Co-givenness and field

    Where appearances present themselves together, their may be described. names this apparent togetherness without positing a container, a unifying entity, or a necessary unity of all . Nearness, distance, depth, separation, and difference are considered as they appear. Their description does not settle the relations of independently existing things. Fragmentation, discontinuity, and uncertainty about what belongs together are equally admissible.

  2. Temporality and recollection

    Succession, duration, persistence, anticipation, and recollection are considered in their respective modes of . A recollection is admitted as recollection without endorsement of the past it presents. An anticipation is admitted without endorsement of the future it projects. Apparent recurrence establishes no necessary law. Historical claims, including claims about the inquiry’s own earlier stages, remain suspended as knowledge claims. This suspension neither establishes their falsity nor demonstrates that historical knowledge is impossible.

  3. Reasoning, evidence, and explanation

    Descriptions, arguments, evidence, and explanations may all be examined and provisionally employed. Their differences are retained: an argument offers reasons, evidence bears on a claim, and an explanation proposes grounds, causes, mechanisms, or conditions. Their occurring within does not by itself validate or invalidate them. Further inquiry may change which account appears better supported without ending suspension. Experiential Immanence claims neither to refute competing accounts of reality nor to establish its own privileged access to truth. Its concepts, inferences, and formulations remain answerable to criticism and revision.

  4. Reflexivity and practice

    Suspension applies to every claim to knowledge, including claims made on behalf of this system. “Only exists,” “nothing can be known,” and “suspension is the uniquely correct method” receive no exemption. Practical reliance does not terminate suspension: recollections, perceptions, and estimates may guide action without being endorsed as knowledge. Remembered success may motivate reliance on memory, but supplies no independent guarantee of its reliability. Such reliance remains revisable in light of further appearances. The practice invites examination of how an anticipation becomes a settled future, an interpretation becomes a fact, or a sense of self becomes an asserted entity. Pain, distress, conviction, and resistance need not disappear or lose their force. The system promises neither relief nor liberation, and supplies no complete rule for reasonable or ethical action.
